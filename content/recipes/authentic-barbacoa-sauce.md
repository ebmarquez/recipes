A deeply flavored, Mexican-style dried-chile barbacoa sauce perfect for beef cheeks, chuck roast,
short ribs, lamb, or goat.

---

## Ingredients

### **Dried Chiles**

- 4 dried **guajillo** chiles
- 2 dried **ancho** chiles
- 2 dried **pasilla** chiles
- 1–2 **chipotle chiles in adobo** (optional but recommended)

### **Aromatics**

- ½ white onion
- 4 garlic cloves
- 1 Roma tomato _(or 2 tbsp tomato paste)_
- 2 bay leaves

### **Seasoning**

- 1 tbsp Mexican oregano
- 1 tsp cumin
- ½ tsp ground clove _(or 1 whole clove)_
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 1–2 tsp kosher salt
- Optional: ½ tsp cinnamon

### **Liquid**

- Hot water for soaking the dried chiles, enough to cover
- 2 cups beef broth
- ¼ cup apple cider vinegar _(or white vinegar)_
- Juice of 1 lime
- 1 tbsp brown sugar or piloncillo

### **Fat**

- 1–2 tbsp neutral oil or beef tallow

---

## Instructions

### Step 1: Toast the Dried Chiles

Remove stems and seeds.

- Toast lightly in a dry skillet for **10–15 seconds per side** until fragrant.
- Avoid burning — it makes the sauce bitter.

### Step 2: Rehydrate

Place chiles in a bowl and cover with hot water.

- Soak **20 minutes** until fully softened.

### Step 3: Build the Flavor Base

Use part of the listed 1-2 tbsp oil or tallow to sauté onion and garlic until browned; reserve the
rest for frying the sauce.

- Add tomato or tomato paste and cook until it darkens.
- Add cumin, oregano, pepper, clove, and the optional cinnamon; bloom for 30 seconds.

### Step 4: Blend the Sauce

Add to a blender:

- Rehydrated chiles
- Sautéed aromatics
- Beef broth
- Vinegar
- Lime juice
- Brown sugar
- Chipotles in adobo (if using)

Blend until **smooth and velvety**.

### Step 5: Strain

Push through a fine mesh strainer for a silky texture.

### Step 6: Fry the Sauce

Heat the reserved oil or tallow in a pot.

- Pour in the sauce — it should sizzle.
- Simmer **10–15 minutes** until thickened and deep red.

### Step 7: Adjust to Taste

More vinegar → brightness

- Add the listed 1-2 tsp kosher salt to taste; more salt adds depth.
- More sugar → balance
- More broth → thinner consistency

---

## Using the Sauce for Barbacoa

Pour the sauce over **3–4 lbs** of:

- Beef cheeks
- Chuck roast
- Short ribs
- Lamb shoulder
- Goat

Add:

- 2 bay leaves
- Extra broth if needed

### **Cooking Times**

- **Instant Pot:** 60–75 minutes
- **Slow Cooker:** 6–8 hours
- **Oven Braise:** 3–4 hours at 300°F

Shred the meat, mix with the sauce, and rest 10 minutes before serving.

## Serving and Heat

**Heat:** Medium-High.

**Suggested pairings:** Beef Cheeks, Chuck Roast, Short Ribs, Lamb Shoulder, Goat.

## Meat-Cooking Notes

The 45-minute total is for the sauce only. The 2 bay leaves are for the meat braise, not the
blender. Use the specified 3-4 lbs of meat; the listed appliance times are estimates, not complete
appliance programs. For pressure cooking, follow the cooker instructions for minimum liquid, fill
limits, pressure setting and safe release; pressurization and release add time. Keep braises covered
and cook until the meat shreds easily.

## Cooking Safety

Whole beef, lamb or goat cuts need at least 145°F plus a 3-minute rest; barbacoa generally needs
longer cooking to become shreddable. Keep raw meat and its utensils separate from ready-to-eat
foods; wash hands and clean work surfaces after handling raw meat.

[Safe cooking temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).

## Dietary Notes

Check broth and canned chipotles; beef broth or tallow means this sauce is not vegetarian. Check
ingredient labels and cross-contact information for allergies.

## Safe Handling

Refrigerate promptly in shallow containers at 40°F or below, within 2 hours of preparation (1 hour
above 90°F). Use within 3-4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165°F; bring soups and sauces to a rolling
boil when reheating. Thaw frozen portions in the refrigerator, not on the counter.

[USDA leftover handling guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety).

## Blending Caution

For a countertop blender, let hot sauce cool to the temperature allowed by the manufacturer; never
seal hot liquid in a personal blender. Blend in small batches with the lid vented as directed. An
immersion blender can avoid transferring the hot sauce; keep its blade submerged.

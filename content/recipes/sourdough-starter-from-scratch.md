A wild-yeast sourdough starter you grow yourself from just flour and water. No store-bought yeast or
packets needed — you're capturing the wild yeast and bacteria already living on the flour and in
your kitchen. Takes about **7 days** to get bubbly and bake-ready (sometimes 10–14 in cold
kitchens).

**Yield:** One active starter you keep alive indefinitely **Active time:** ~5 min/day **Total to
first bake:** 7–14 days

---

## Ingredients

- **Flour: 60g per feeding** — unbleached all-purpose works, but **whole wheat or rye** kickstarts
  it faster (more wild yeast + nutrients). Best combo: start with rye/whole wheat for the first few
  days, then transition to all-purpose or bread flour.
- **Water: 60g per feeding** — filtered or dechlorinated. Chlorine in tap water can stunt the yeast.
  If your tap water inhibits activity, use filtered water; standing does not remove every
  disinfectant, including chloramine.

## Equipment

- A clear glass jar or container (1 qt / ~1L), loosely covered (lid resting on top, NOT sealed
  airtight — it needs to breathe and will produce gas)
- Kitchen scale (strongly recommended — sourdough is a ratio game; volume measuring is unreliable)
- A spoon or small spatula
- A rubber band or marker to mark the jar level

**No scale?** Rough volume equivalents: 1/2 cup flour ≈ 60g, 1/4 cup water ≈ 60g. But get a scale if
you can — it makes everything consistent.

---

## Instructions

The whole game is: **feed it equal parts flour and water by weight, every day, and discard most of
it before each feeding.** The discard prevents it from overflowing and keeps the food-to-microbe
ratio healthy.

### Step 1: Day 1 — Mix

In your jar, combine **60g whole wheat or rye flour** + **60g water** (room temp).

- Stir into a thick paste, scrape down the sides.
- Mark the level with a rubber band. Cover loosely.
- Leave somewhere warm (70–78°F / 21–25°C is ideal — a warm cupboard, or an off oven only if checked
  to stay within that range).

### Step 2: Days 2–3 — First feedings

You may see a few bubbles, or nothing yet. Both are fine.

- **Discard** all but about **60g** of the starter (eyeball ~1/4 cup; throw the early discard away;
  do not save it for food).
- Feed the remaining: add **60g flour** + **60g water**, stir, re-mark the level, cover.
- Once a day is fine for now.

> **Early activity is not readiness:** A brief rise in the first few days does not establish a
> mature starter. Keep feeding, discard early excess, and throw the whole starter away if mold or
> pink/orange discoloration appears. Smell alone cannot establish safety.

### Step 3: Days 4–6 — Twice-daily feeding

By now it should smell **sour/tangy/yeasty** (a good sign) and start rising more predictably.

- Switch to feeding **twice a day** (roughly every 12 hours) to build strength.
- Each feeding: discard down to ~60g, then add **60g flour + 60g water**.
- You can now transition to **all-purpose or bread flour** if you started with rye/wheat.
- Watch it: it should start to **roughly double in size** between feedings and be full of bubbles.

### Step 4: Day 7+ — Test if it's ready

Your starter is **ready to bake** when it reliably:

1. **Doubles in size** within 4–8 hours of feeding, AND
2. Is **bubbly and domed** on top, AND
3. Smells pleasantly sour/tangy (like yogurt or beer), not rotten.

**Optional float check:** A spoonful may float when it contains enough gas, but this alone does not
prove maturity or safety. Use reliable rise after repeated feedings as the baking-readiness cue.

---

## Once It's Alive — Keeping It

**Baking regularly (a few times a week):**

- Keep it on the counter, feed once or twice daily as above.

**Baking occasionally:**

- Store it in the **fridge** and feed just **once a week**. The cold slows everything down.
- To bake: pull it out, feed it, leave on the counter, and let it wake up / get bubbly for a feeding
  cycle or two before using.

**Hooch on an established starter:** Liquid can indicate a hungry starter. Feed regularly, but
discard the entire starter if there is mold or pink/orange discoloration; liquid color alone does
not certify safety.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem                           | What it means / fix                                                                          |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No bubbles after 3–4 days         | Too cold — move somewhere warmer. Try switching to rye/whole wheat. Be patient.              |
| Pink, orange, or fuzzy mold       | ❌ **Throw it out and start over.** Real mold = done. Do not try to salvage a moldy starter. |
| Smells like nail polish / acetone | It's hungry — feed it more often or use more flour per feeding.                              |
| Rises then collapses fast         | It's peaking and over-fermenting — use it sooner after feeding, or feed more.                |
| Stalled / sluggish                | Feed twice daily, keep warm, switch in some whole grain flour for a boost.                   |

---

## What to Do With Discard

Do not put thick discard down the drain. Discard the excess during initial establishment. Only save
discard from a healthy, established starter with reliable feeding activity; refrigerate it promptly
and use it within 3-4 days or freeze. Use it in fully cooked pancakes, waffles, crackers, banana
bread, pizza dough or biscuits, never raw.

---

## Pro Tips

- **Name it.** Seriously — people name their starters. It builds the habit of caring for it daily.
  (Classic names: Clint Yeastwood, Bread Pitt, Doughy Parton.)
- **Consistency beats perfection.** Feeding at roughly the same times daily makes it predictable and
  strong.
- **Warmth is the #1 accelerator.** A cold kitchen is the most common reason a starter is slow.
  Check the actual temperature before using an off oven with its light on; never preheat it with the
  starter inside.
- **Keep a backup.** Once it's thriving, dry some out (smear thin, let dry, crumble, store in a jar)
  as insurance in case the live one dies.

## Starter and Flour Safety

Never taste raw starter, discard or flour dough. Wash hands, jars and tools; do not use appearance
or smell alone as proof of safety.

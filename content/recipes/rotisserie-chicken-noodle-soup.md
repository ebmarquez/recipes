Classic comfort soup with a shortcut: a rotisserie chicken provides deep, seasoned flavor in a
fraction of the time. A butter-and-flour roux thickens the broth into something closer to a
gravy-style soup than a thin broth.

**Prep:** 10 minutes | **Cook:** 25 minutes | **Total:** About 35 minutes; add 30 minutes for
optional carcass simmer | **Yield:** 6-8 servings

## Ingredients

- 1 rotisserie chicken (shred all the meat, about 3 cups)
- 4 tbsp butter
- 4 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 1 large onion, diced
- 3-4 carrots, sliced
- 3-4 celery stalks, sliced
- 4-5 cloves garlic, minced
- 8 cups chicken broth
- 2 cups water
- 8 oz wide egg noodles
- 2 bay leaves
- Salt, pepper, dried thyme, and parsley to taste

## Instructions

1. **Shred the chicken** — pull all the meat off the rotisserie chicken. Set aside.
2. **Start the roux:** Melt butter in a large pot over medium heat.
3. **Cook the vegetables:** Add onion, carrots, and celery. Sauté 5-6 minutes until softened. Add
   garlic and cook 1 minute.
4. **Build the roux:** Sprinkle flour over the vegetables, stir for 2 minutes. This thickens the
   broth.
5. **Add liquid:** Slowly pour in broth and water, stirring to prevent lumps. Add bay leaves, thyme,
   salt, and pepper. Bring to a boil.
6. **Cook noodles:** Add egg noodles and cook 8-10 minutes until tender.
7. **Add chicken:** Stir in the shredded chicken and heat to 165°F, allowing more than 2-3 minutes
   if needed. Remove bay leaves. Taste and adjust seasoning; finish with the parsley.

## Notes

- **Maximum flavor:** Simmer the chicken carcass in the broth and water for 30 minutes before
  starting, then strain it out. This optional step adds 30 minutes.
- **Meal prep:** Stores 3-4 days in the fridge. Keep noodles separate to avoid them getting mushy.
- Swap egg noodles for ditalini or orzo for variety.

## Safe Handling

Refrigerate promptly in shallow containers at 40°F or below, within 2 hours of preparation (1 hour
above 90°F). Use within 3-4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165°F; bring soups and sauces to a rolling
boil when reheating. Thaw frozen portions in the refrigerator, not on the counter.

[USDA leftover handling guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety).

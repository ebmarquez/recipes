Make a gallon-size batch of tangy yogurt with whole milk and a live-culture
starter. Enjoy it chilled as regular yogurt, or strain it in the refrigerator
for a thicker Greek-style batch.

## Equipment

- Instant Pot IP-DUO or another Instant Pot with a temperature-controlled
  Yogurt mode and enough capacity for 1 gallon of milk within the model's
  yogurt-program fill limit
- Food thermometer
- Whisk and a clean bowl
- Ice bath large enough for the inner pot
- Clean, shallow food-safe containers with lids
- Fine-mesh strainer, a large bowl, and cheesecloth or a nut milk bag for
  Greek yogurt

## Ingredients

- 1 gallon pasteurized whole milk
- 2 tablespoons plain Greek yogurt with live active cultures, such as
  Kirkland Organic Greek Yogurt; check the label

### Optional

- 2 tablespoons nonfat dry milk powder for extra thickness

## Instructions

### Step 1: Heat the Milk

Use the yogurt heating program and verify the milk temperature directly.

1. Wash the pot, lid, thermometer, utensils, and containers thoroughly.
   Follow the manufacturer's cleaning and sanitizing instructions. Check the
   exact model's yogurt instructions and capacity before starting.
2. Pour the milk into the inner pot. Whisk in the optional milk powder
   until completely dissolved.
3. Select the yogurt heating program, labeled **BOIL** on compatible models,
   and allow it to finish. This is not a pressure-cooking cycle.
4. Stir and check the milk with the thermometer; it should reach
   180-185 degrees F (82-85 degrees C). Do not rely on the completion beep
   alone. If needed, use the model's recommended additional heating method,
   stirring and monitoring to prevent scorching.

### Step 2: Cool the Milk

Cool the milk before adding the live-culture starter.

1. Carefully remove the inner pot and set it in an ice bath, keeping bath
   water out of the milk.
2. Stir occasionally and check the temperature until the milk reaches
   110 degrees F (43 degrees C).
3. Dry the outside and base of the inner pot before returning it to the cooker.

### Step 3: Add the Starter

Blend the starter with a little cooled milk so it mixes evenly.

1. Transfer 1 cup of the cooled milk to a clean bowl.
2. Whisk in the 2 tablespoons of yogurt starter until smooth.
3. Pour the mixture back into the pot and stir gently to distribute it.

### Step 4: Incubate

Culture the milk under controlled temperature, without pressure cooking.

1. Return the inner pot to the Instant Pot and fit the lid according to the
   model's yogurt instructions. This cycle does not use pressure; on the
   IP-DUO, the pressure valve position does not matter.
2. Select the **Yogurt** incubation setting, not **BOIL**, Keep Warm, or a
   pressure-cooking program. Maintain approximately 110 degrees F
   (43 degrees C), within 105-115 degrees F (41-46 degrees C), using the
   model's temperature-controlled yogurt program.
3. Choose an incubation time from the guide below. Start with 10 hours for
   a balance of tang and texture; results depend on the milk and culture.
4. Leave the yogurt undisturbed during incubation. If temperature control
   fails or the yogurt does not set, discard the batch without tasting;
   do not extend incubation indefinitely to rescue it.

| Time | Expected flavor |
| --- | --- |
| 8 hours | Mild |
| 10 hours | Balanced |
| 12 hours | Tangy |

### Step 5: Chill

Refrigerate promptly in shallow containers for faster cooling.

1. At the end of incubation, promptly transfer the yogurt into clean,
   shallow food-safe containers rather than cooling the entire gallon in
   one deep pot.
2. Cover and refrigerate at 40 degrees F (4 degrees C) or below for at least
   6 hours or overnight.
3. Avoid stirring during chilling so the yogurt can firm up.

## To Make Greek Yogurt

1. Line the fine-mesh strainer with clean cheesecloth or a nut milk bag
   and set it over a large bowl.
2. Gently transfer the chilled yogurt into the lined strainer.
3. Cover and refrigerate while it drains, using the guide below. Keep the
   yogurt above the collected whey; work in batches if necessary.
4. Transfer the strained yogurt to clean, covered containers. Reserve
   the whey in a separate covered container in the refrigerator if desired.

| Draining time | Expected texture |
| --- | --- |
| 1 hour | Thick yogurt |
| 2-3 hours | Greek yogurt |
| 4+ hours | Extra-thick Greek yogurt |

## Notes

- Whole milk gives the creamiest result. The final strained yield varies
  with draining time.
- Refrigerated whey can be used in smoothies, bread, pancakes, or protein
  shakes.
- To reuse a starter, reserve 2 tablespoons from a fresh batch in a clean,
  covered container before serving. Repeated batches can lose culture
  strength; use fresh store-bought live-culture yogurt if setting becomes
  unreliable.
- If the sealing ring carries savory odors, follow the model's yogurt
  instructions for removing it or using a separate clean ring. Do not
  remove a ring required by the manufacturer's instructions.
- Check milk, starter, and milk-powder labels for allergens and additives.
- Temperature control and prompt refrigeration are essential. See
  [University of Illinois Extension's home yogurt guide](https://extension.illinois.edu/media/8085/download)
  for additional handling guidance; the owner supplied this recipe.

## Storage

- Following
  [University of Illinois Extension's home yogurt guidance](https://extension.illinois.edu/media/8085/download),
  use homemade yogurt within **14 days (2 weeks)** of making it when kept
  refrigerated at **40 degrees F (4 degrees C) or below**.
- Refrigerate promptly after incubation and keep covered in clean containers.
  Use clean utensils each time. The 14-day window assumes proper preparation
  and continuous refrigeration; it does not make an improperly handled batch safe.
- Keep reserved starter and whey refrigerated too; straining does not
  restart the storage clock.
- Discard yogurt with mold or other spoilage. Normal appearance or smell
  does not establish safety after improper temperature control.
- Serve cold; reheating is not needed.

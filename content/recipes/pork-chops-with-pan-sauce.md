Perfectly seared bone-in pork chops with a quick mustard-herb pan sauce made from the fond in the
skillet. Classic comfort food, pairs with any side you've got — mashed potatoes, roasted veg, rice,
whatever's in the fridge.

**Prep:** 5 minutes | **Cook:** 20 minutes | **Total:** 25 minutes | **Yield:** 4 servings

## Ingredients

- 4 bone-in pork chops (1 inch thick)
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 shallot, finely diced (or ¼ cup diced onion)
- ½ cup chicken broth
- 2 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tbsp whole grain mustard (optional, adds texture)
- 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves (or 1 tsp dried)
- 1 tbsp fresh rosemary, chopped (or 1 tsp dried)
- Splash of white wine or apple cider vinegar
- Kosher salt & black pepper

## Instructions

1. **Prep the chops** — Pat pork chops dry. Season generously with salt and pepper on both sides.
   Let them sit at room temp while you prep the other ingredients.
2. **Sear the chops (8 min)** — Large skillet, medium-high heat, 2 tbsp olive oil. When the oil
   shimmers, lay the chops in. Cook 4 min per side until golden brown with a nice crust. Check
   internal temp — cook to at least 145°F before removing from the heat. Rest on a clean plate for
   at least 3 minutes, tented with foil, while making the sauce.
3. **Build the sauce (5 min)** — Same skillet, reduce to medium heat. Add butter and shallot, cook 2
   min until softened. Add garlic, cook 30 seconds. Pour in chicken broth and wine/vinegar — scrape
   up all the fond (brown bits) from the bottom. This is pure flavor.
4. **Finish the sauce** — Stir in Dijon mustard, whole grain mustard, thyme, and rosemary. Let it
   simmer 2-3 min until slightly thickened. Taste and adjust salt/pepper.
5. **Serve** — Nestle the chops back into the pan, spoon sauce over the top. Serve immediately with
   your choice of sides.

## Chef's Tips

- **Dry the chops** — wet meat doesn't sear, it steams. Paper towels, both sides, every time.
- **Don't skip the rest** — 5 min rest under foil. Cut too early and all the juice runs out onto
  your cutting board instead of staying in the meat.
- **The fond is the sauce** — those brown bits stuck to the pan are concentrated flavor. The broth
  lifts them off. Don't wash the pan between searing and sauce-making.
- **Bone-in, thick cut** — bone-in chops stay juicier. 1 inch thick minimum. Thin chops overcook in
  seconds.
- **Reheating:** Low oven (300°F) until the chops reach 165°F; allow more than 10 minutes if needed.
  The sauce reheats perfectly — add a splash of broth if it's too thick.

## Variations

- **Creamy version** → add 2 tbsp heavy cream to the sauce at the end
- **Apple cider** → replace broth with apple cider for a fall twist
- **Mushroom** → sauté sliced mushrooms with the shallot before deglazing
- **Honey mustard** → add 1 tbsp honey to the sauce for sweet/savory

## Dietary Notes

Butter is dairy; the cream variation adds more. Check broth and mustard labels. Check ingredient
labels and cross-contact information for allergies.

## Cooking Safety

Whole pork chops must reach 145°F before leaving the heat, followed by at least a 3-minute rest; the
original 5-minute rest also meets that minimum. Keep raw meat and its utensils separate from
ready-to-eat foods; wash hands and clean work surfaces after handling raw meat.

[Safe cooking temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).

## Safe Handling

Refrigerate promptly in shallow containers at 40°F or below, within 2 hours of preparation (1 hour
above 90°F). Use within 3-4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165°F; bring soups and sauces to a rolling
boil when reheating. Thaw frozen portions in the refrigerator, not on the counter.

[USDA leftover handling guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety).

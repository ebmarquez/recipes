_Pronounced "wah-ZAHN-kee."_ A rustic Polish comfort dish — smoky kielbasa, golden onions, sautéed
cabbage, and tangy sauerkraut tossed with square noodles. Almost no sauce, huge flavor from cheap
ingredients, and it's even better as leftovers on day 2-3. Pasta shape and parallel cooking affect
the total time.

**Prep:** 10 minutes | **Cook:** About 20-25 minutes skillet cooking, plus pasta and final toss |
**Total:** About 30-35 minutes or longer; pasta may cook alongside the skillet | **Yield:** 3-4
servings

## Ingredients

- 12 oz fully cooked kielbasa (smoked Polish sausage), sliced into ¼" rounds
- 1 large onion, halved and thinly sliced into half-moons
- ½ small head green cabbage (~4-5 cups), shredded
- ¾ cup sauerkraut, drained (squeeze out excess liquid), roughly chopped
- 8 oz pasta — traditional łazanki squares, or **substitute: farfalle (bowtie), egg noodles, or
  pappardelle cut into squares**
- 2-3 tbsp oil (or butter; lard for max flavor)
- ½ tsp salt, ½ tsp black pepper
- Optional: 4 oz cremini mushrooms, sliced (very common addition)
- Optional: pat of butter to finish

## Instructions

1. Bring a pot of salted water to a boil. Cook pasta until just al dente (łazanki squares cook fast,
   ~4-5 min; bowties ~10 min). Drain and set aside.
2. Heat oil in a large 12" skillet over medium-high. Add the sliced kielbasa and fry until browned
   and crisp at the edges, 4-5 min. Remove and set aside.
3. In the same pan (use the rendered fat), add onions and cook until soft and golden, 6-8 min. (If
   using mushrooms, add them with the onions and cook until browned.)
4. Add the shredded cabbage. Cook, stirring, until it softens and lightly browns — it'll cook down
   to about a third of its volume, ~8-10 min.
5. Stir in the chopped sauerkraut and heat through, 2 min. Season with salt and pepper.
6. Return the kielbasa to the pan, add the drained pasta, and toss everything together until hot and
   well combined. Finish with a pat of butter if you like.
7. Taste and adjust salt/pepper. Serve hot.

## Chef's Tips

- **Can't find łazanki squares?** Farfalle (bowties) are the easiest swap and hold the chunky bits
  well. Egg noodles or hand-cut pappardelle squares also work.
- **Cabbage + sauerkraut together** turns the flavor dial all the way up — fresh cabbage for
  sweetness, kraut for tang. You can use all of one or the other, but both is best.
- **Brown the kielbasa hard** — those crispy edges and rendered smoky fat are what flavor the whole
  dish.
- **Leftovers rule:** Keeps 3-4 days in the fridge and tastes even better on day 2. Great cold as a
  lunchbox pasta salad. Don't freeze.
- **Want it richer?** Fry the onions in lard or bacon fat, or add a few strips of chopped bacon with
  the kielbasa.
- Serve with extra sauerkraut on the side and some crusty or cheesy bread.

## Dietary Notes

Use a fully cooked kielbasa and check its label. Butter, egg noodles and serving breads change
allergen content. Check ingredient labels and cross-contact information for allergies.

## Safe Handling

Refrigerate promptly in shallow containers at 40°F or below, within 2 hours of preparation (1 hour
above 90°F). Use within 3-4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165°F; bring soups and sauces to a rolling
boil when reheating. Thaw frozen portions in the refrigerator, not on the counter.

[USDA leftover handling guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety).

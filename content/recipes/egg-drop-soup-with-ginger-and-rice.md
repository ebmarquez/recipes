Light, warm soup with ginger and silky egg ribbons. The 15-minute estimate assumes the rice is
already cooked.

**Prep:** 5 minutes | **Cook:** 10 minutes | **Total:** 15 minutes | **Yield:** 4 servings

## Ingredients

- 4 cups chicken broth
- 3 eggs, beaten
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, minced (or 1 tsp ground ginger)
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 2 green onions, thinly sliced
- 1 tsp cornstarch + 1 tbsp cold water (optional, for silkier texture)
- Steamed rice for serving
- White pepper to taste

## Instructions

1. **Bring broth to a simmer** over medium heat. Add ginger and soy sauce. Simmer 3-4 min to infuse.
2. **Optional:** stir in cornstarch slurry for a slightly thicker, silkier broth.
3. **Drizzle in beaten eggs** slowly in a thin stream while gently stirring the soup in one
   direction. The eggs will form silky ribbons. Don't stir too aggressively.
4. **Check doneness:** Keep at a gentle simmer until the eggs are set and the soup reaches 160°F.
   Then remove from heat. Drizzle in sesame oil, add white pepper to taste.
5. **Serve** over steamed rice, topped with sliced green onions.

## Chef's Tips

- **Thin stream = silky ribbons.** Pour the eggs from a fork or measuring cup with a spout for
  control.
- **Don't boil** — a gentle simmer gives you delicate ribbons. A rolling boil gives you scrambled
  egg soup.
- **Add-ins:** shredded rotisserie chicken, baby spinach, corn, or tofu for more substance.
- **Keeps well** — refrigerate up to 3 days. Reheat gently on the stove.

## Variations

- **Spicy** → add chili oil or sriracha
- **Heartier** → add shredded chicken and baby corn
- **Thai twist** → swap ginger for lemongrass, add a splash of coconut milk and lime

## Dietary Notes

Ordinary soy sauce often contains wheat. Use verified gluten-free soy sauce or tamari and broth for
that variation. Check ingredient labels and cross-contact information for allergies.

## Safe Handling

Refrigerate promptly in shallow containers at 40°F or below, within 2 hours of preparation (1 hour
above 90°F). Use within 3 days. Reheat leftovers to 165°F; bring soups and sauces to a rolling boil
when reheating. Thaw frozen portions in the refrigerator, not on the counter.

[USDA leftover handling guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety).

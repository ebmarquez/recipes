Hot vinegar brine softens sliced onions into a sharp refrigerated condiment for tacos and
sandwiches.

## Ingredients

- 2-3 red onions, thinly sliced
- A few additional peppercorns for the containers
- 6-7 garlic cloves total; thinly slice 1 for the onions and use the rest in the brine
- 2⅔ cups red wine vinegar, labeled 5% acidity
- 1⅓ cups water
- 1½ tbsp peppercorns for the brine
- 1½ tbsp sugar
- 1½ tbsp salt

## Instructions

1. **Choose enough containers.** Use clean, heat-safe containers with room for both the onions and
   about 4 cups of brine; one quart jar will not hold both. Divide the separated onion slices, the
   sliced garlic clove and the few extra peppercorns among them.
2. **Heat the brine.** Combine the vinegar, water, measured peppercorns, sugar, salt and remaining
   garlic in a saucepan. Stir to dissolve the crystals and bring to a full boil.
3. **Cover the onions.** Carefully pour the hot brine over them. Let the containers stand briefly,
   about 30 minutes, then refrigerate; do not wait for the whole batch to cool to room temperature.
   Avoid thermal shock to glass and follow the container manufacturer's hot-fill guidance.
4. **Rest under refrigeration.** Wait at least 2 hours before serving, or up to 24 hours for a
   stronger pickle. Keep the onions submerged and use a clean utensil to take out a portion.

## Notes

- The recorded yield is about 4 cups of brine, not 4 cups of finished onions.
- This is a refrigerator recipe, not a water-bath canning method. Hot brine does not make a sealed
  jar safe for pantry storage.
- Use a fresh batch of brine for new onions. Used brine can be added to a dressing only while it
  remains within the same refrigerated storage window; do not treat it as a renewed preservation
  liquid.
- Serve with [Soy Chorizo and Potato Enchiladas](../soy-chorizo-and-potato-enchiladas/) or
  [Roasted Vegetable Enchiladas](../roasted-veggie-and-black-bean-enchiladas/).
- Check vinegar labels for dietary restrictions.

## Storage

Refrigerate leftovers in shallow covered containers at 40°F or below within 2 hours of cooking or
preparation, or within 1 hour when the surroundings exceed 90°F. Use within 3-4 days. Freezing is
not recommended because the onion texture softens.

[USDA cooling, storage and reheating guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety).

Spicy, crumbly Mexican chorizo meets caramelized sweet potato cubes — sweet, savory, smoky all in
one bite. The sweet potato mellows the heat, the pickled onions cut through the richness, and the
crema ties it all together.

**Prep:** 10 minutes | **Cook:** 15 minutes | **Total:** 25 minutes | **Yield:** 4 servings (2-3
tacos each)

## Ingredients

- 12 oz Mexican chorizo (raw, loose — not cured Spanish chorizo)
- 2 medium sweet potatoes, peeled, ½" dice
- 8-12 corn tortillas
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- ½ tsp cumin
- ½ tsp smoked paprika
- 2 limes
- Kosher salt

**Substitutions:** Ground pork + 1 tbsp chili powder + 1 tsp cumin + ½ tsp paprika + splash of
vinegar for the chorizo. Butternut squash cubes for sweet potato. Small flour tortillas for corn.

### Toppings

- Mexican crema or sour cream (thin with a splash of lime juice)
- Cotija cheese, crumbled (feta works in a pinch)
- Fresh cilantro, generous handful
- Pickled red onions (see below)
- Avocado, sliced or smashed
- Hot sauce (Valentina, Cholula, or your go-to)

### Quick Pickled Red Onions

- 1 red onion, thinly sliced
- ½ cup rice vinegar
- 1 tsp sugar
- ½ tsp salt

Toss in a jar, shake, let sit while you cook. Ready by taco time. Refrigerate promptly and use
within 3-4 days; these quick onions are not shelf-stable or a canning recipe.

## Instructions

1. **Cook the sweet potato (10 min)** — Large skillet, medium-high heat, 2 tbsp olive oil. Add diced
   sweet potato in a single layer. Season with cumin, smoked paprika, salt. Cook 8-10 min, stirring
   every 2-3 min, until golden and fork-tender. Remove to a bowl.
2. **Cook the chorizo (5 min)** — Same skillet, no extra oil needed (chorizo has plenty of fat).
   Squeeze chorizo from casing, break it up with a spoon. Add garlic. Cook for about 4-5 minutes or
   longer until browned and at least 160°F internally (165°F for poultry chorizo). Drain any excess
   grease.
3. **Combine** — Toss the sweet potato back into the skillet with the chorizo. Squeeze half a lime
   over everything, toss gently.
4. **Warm the tortillas** — Directly over a gas flame (15 sec per side) or in a dry skillet until
   pliable and lightly charred.
5. **Build the tacos** — Spoon chorizo-sweet potato mix into tortillas. Top with crema, cotija,
   pickled onions, cilantro, avocado, hot sauce if desired, and a squeeze of lime.

## Chef's Tips

- **Dice the sweet potato small** — ½" cubes cook fast and get crispy edges. Bigger chunks take too
  long for a weeknight.
- **Don't stir the sweet potato constantly** — let it sit and develop a crust on the bottom before
  flipping. That caramelization is the whole point.
- **Mexican chorizo ≠ Spanish chorizo** — you want the raw, loose stuff in the casing (usually near
  the ground pork), not the cured hard sausage.
- **Meal prep move** — cook the chorizo & sweet potato filling in bulk. Keeps 4 days in the fridge.
  Reheat in a skillet, assemble fresh tortillas & toppings.
- **Make the pickled onions ahead** — a jar in the fridge means instant taco upgrade any night.

## Variations

- **Black beans** → add a can of drained black beans to the filling for more substance
- **Breakfast tacos** → top with a fried egg in the morning — incredible
- **Kale or spinach** → wilt a handful into the chorizo for some greens
- **Bowl it** → skip tortillas, serve over rice with all the toppings

## Dietary Notes

Check chorizo and sauces for wheat; substituting flour tortillas adds gluten. Crema and cotija
contain milk. Check ingredient labels and cross-contact information for allergies.

## Cooking Safety

Raw pork or beef chorizo and ground-pork substitutions need 160°F; poultry chorizo needs 165°F. Keep
raw meat and its utensils separate from ready-to-eat foods; wash hands and clean work surfaces after
handling raw meat.

[Safe cooking temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).

## Safe Handling

Refrigerate promptly in shallow containers at 40°F or below, within 2 hours of preparation (1 hour
above 90°F). Use within 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165°F; bring soups and sauces to a rolling boil
when reheating. Thaw frozen portions in the refrigerator, not on the counter.

[USDA leftover handling guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety).

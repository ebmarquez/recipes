A soft, flavorful everyday sandwich loaf made with honey, butter, milk powder, and the tangzhong
method. The cooked flour paste helps the bread retain moisture and stale more slowly while keeping
the crumb sturdy enough for sandwiches.

- **Makes:** One 9×5-inch loaf, about 16–18 slices
- **Active time:** About 35–40 minutes
- **Rise:** 1¾–2¾ hours
- **Bake:** 40–45 minutes
- **Cooling:** At least 2 hours
- **Total elapsed time:** About 5 hours 25 minutes to 6 hours 40 minutes

## Ingredients

### Tangzhong

- **25g bread flour**
- **125g whole milk**

### Final Dough

- All of the cooled tangzhong
- **475g bread flour**
- **215g lukewarm water**
- **35g honey**
- **20g nonfat dry milk powder**
- **11g fine salt**
- **7g instant yeast** _(one standard packet)_
- **40g unsalted butter**, softened
- Additional butter or oil for greasing, as needed
- Additional butter for brushing the hot loaf, optional

## Instructions

### Step 1: Make the Tangzhong — 20–25 minutes

_About 3–5 minutes active, followed by 15–20 minutes cooling._

1. Whisk **25g flour** and **125g milk** together in a small saucepan until smooth.
2. Cook over medium-low heat, whisking constantly, until it reaches **65°C/149°F** and thickens to
   the consistency of loose pudding, about 2–4 minutes.
3. Scrape it into the mixing bowl and cover the surface. Let it cool until lukewarm, about 15–20
   minutes. It should not be hot enough to harm the yeast.

### Step 2: Mix the Dough — 30–35 minutes

_Includes the 15-minute rest and 11–15 minutes of kneading._

1. Add the water, honey, yeast, milk powder, and 475g flour to the cooled tangzhong.
2. Mix until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest for **15 minutes**.
3. Add the salt and knead for about **5 minutes**.
4. Add the softened butter a little at a time, kneading until incorporated. Continue kneading until
   the dough is smooth, elastic, and slightly tacky, about **6–10 minutes** in a mixer or **10–15
   minutes** by hand.
5. The dough should stretch thin enough to let light through without immediately tearing—the
   **windowpane test**.

### Step 3: First Rise — 60–90 minutes

_Mostly hands-off; temperature and yeast activity determine the exact time._

1. Place the dough in a lightly greased bowl and cover.
2. Let rise at room temperature until doubled, approximately **60–90 minutes**.

### Step 4: Shape and Proof — 55–85 minutes

_Allow about 10 minutes to shape the loaf and 45–75 minutes for the final proof._

1. Grease a **9×5-inch loaf pan**.
2. Gently press the dough into a rectangle about as wide as the pan.
3. Roll it up tightly from a short end, pinching the final seam closed.
4. Place it seam-side down in the pan and cover loosely.
5. Proof until the center of the loaf rises about **1 inch above the pan rim**, approximately
   **45–75 minutes**. The dough should slowly spring back when gently pressed; if it snaps back
   immediately, give it more time.

### Step 5: Bake — 40–45 minutes, plus at least 2 hours cooling

_Preheat the oven during the final proof so it does not add to the total time._

1. During the final proof, preheat the oven to **350°F**.
2. Bake the loaf in the metal pan for **40 minutes**, rotating the pan once after 25 minutes if the
   oven browns unevenly.
3. At 40 minutes, check the top and the center while leaving the loaf in the pan. The top should be
   evenly golden and the center set, not wet or gummy; a thin skewer inserted into the center should
   not emerge coated with raw dough. If it is pale or the center is not set, bake for another **5
   minutes** and check again, continuing as needed. Do not remove and reinsert the hot loaf just to
   test it.
4. When the bake is finished, use oven mitts to remove the loaf from the pan once and place it on a
   cooling rack. For a softer crust, brush the hot loaf lightly with butter.
5. Cool completely on a rack for at least **2 hours** before slicing.

## Storage

- Store completely cooled bread in an airtight bag or bread box at room temperature for **3–4
  days**.
- Do not refrigerate; refrigeration makes bread stale faster.
- For longer storage, slice the loaf and freeze it. Toast individual slices directly from frozen.

## Flour Safety

Do not taste raw dough or tangzhong; flour is a raw ingredient. Bake the loaf fully before eating.

[Raw dough guidance](https://www.foodsafety.gov/blog/say-no-raw-dough).

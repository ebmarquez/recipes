Finely diced onion, carrot and celery form the base of this meat sauce. Milk
and white wine cook down in separate stages before the tomatoes go in for
a long, gentle simmer.

## Ingredients

- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tbsp butter
- 1 onion, finely diced
- 2 carrots, finely diced
- 2 celery stalks, finely diced
- 1 lb ground beef, or a ground beef and pork mixture
- 1 cup whole milk
- 1 cup dry white wine
- 1 can (28 oz) crushed tomatoes
- 1 tsp salt for the sauce
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 lb tagliatelle or spaghetti
- Water for boiling the pasta and loosening the sauce as needed
- Additional salt for the pasta cooking water

## Instructions

1. **Soften the vegetables.** Set a large, heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat.
   Add the olive oil and butter, then the onion, carrots and celery. Stir
   occasionally for 8-10 minutes, until the vegetables soften. Lower the heat
   if they begin to scorch.
2. **Brown the meat.** Add the ground beef or beef and pork mixture. Break it
   into small pieces and cook over medium-high heat, letting released moisture
   evaporate so the meat can brown. Stir and scrape the bottom as needed to
   protect the vegetables. Add the 1 tsp salt and the black pepper. Ground
   beef or a beef and pork mixture must reach 160 F; color alone is not a
   reliable doneness check.
3. **Reduce the milk.** Pour in the whole milk and lower the heat to maintain
   a gentle simmer. Stir frequently, scraping up browned bits, until little
   free liquid remains. Do not let the milk solids burn on the bottom.
4. **Reduce the wine.** Stir in the dry white wine. Simmer until the added
   liquid has reduced by roughly half.
5. **Add the tomatoes.** Stir in the crushed tomatoes and bay leaf. Bring the
   sauce to a simmer, then reduce the heat to low.
6. **Simmer slowly.** Leave the pot uncovered for 1 1/2-2 hours, stirring
   occasionally and scraping the bottom. Aim for occasional small bubbles,
   not a rolling boil. The finished sauce should be thick enough to coat
   the pasta without a pool of watery liquid. If it dries out before the
   simmer is complete, add a little water and lower the heat.
7. **Boil the pasta.** Near the end of the sauce's cooking time, bring a
   separate large pot of water to a boil and salt it. Cook the tagliatelle
   or spaghetti according to its package directions until al dente.
   Reserve 1/2 cup of the cooking water before draining.
8. **Combine and serve.** Remove and discard the bay leaf. Toss the drained
   pasta with the sauce over low heat. Add small splashes of the reserved
   pasta water only as needed to help the sauce coat the strands. Divide
   among six servings.

## Notes

- **Timing:** The supplied cooking estimate is two hours, but the final
  simmer alone takes 1 1/2-2 hours. Browning and reducing the milk and wine
  take additional time, so allow longer rather than rushing those stages.
- **Meat choice:** Use the full 1 lb of either ground beef or a beef and
  pork mixture. The quantities of the remaining ingredients stay the same.
- **Budget:** Dried spaghetti is an alternative to tagliatelle using the
  same 1 lb quantity. The sauce can be portioned and frozen for another meal.
- **Allergens and alcohol:** Milk and butter contain dairy; ordinary pasta
  contains wheat, and some tagliatelle contains egg. Check labels for other
  allergens. Reducing the wine does not make the sauce alcohol-free.
- **Food safety:** Keep raw meat, its juices and utensils away from cooked
  pasta and ready-to-eat foods. Wash hands and clean preparation surfaces.

## Make-Ahead and Storage

- **Refrigerate:** Divide sauce and pasta into shallow containers and
  refrigerate within 2 hours, or within 1 hour above 90 F. Keep at 40 F or
  below and use within 3-4 days. Store separately when possible so the
  pasta does not absorb all the sauce.
- **Freeze:** Cool safely, then freeze the sauce in meal-size portions in
  freezer-safe containers for up to 3 months for best quality. Cook fresh
  pasta when serving. Thaw sauce in the refrigerator, not on the counter.
- **Reheat:** Heat gently in a saucepan, stirring and adding a little water
  if needed. Bring leftover sauce to a rolling boil and confirm it reaches
  165 F throughout. Reheat any stored pasta to 165 F as well.

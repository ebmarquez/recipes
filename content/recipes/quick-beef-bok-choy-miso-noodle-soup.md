Warm, savory, and ready in about 20 minutes. Thin shabu-shabu beef cooks quickly in the
broth, while bok choy adds freshness and the noodles make this a satisfying family dinner.
For a hard workout later, serve it 2-3 hours beforehand and keep the optional oil and chili
light for an easier meal.

**Prep:** 5 minutes | **Cook:** 15 minutes | **Total:** 20 minutes | **Yield:** 4 servings

## Ingredients

- 1 1/2 lbs frozen shabu-shabu beef strips
- 10-12 oz dry Japanese noodles
- 4-6 heads bok choy, sliced with stems and leaves separated
- 10 cups water
- 6 tbsp miso paste
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, finely grated (or 1 tsp ground ginger)
- 3 cloves garlic, finely minced
- 1-2 tbsp soy sauce, to taste
- 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
- Cooked rice, optional, instead of noodles
- Chili crisp or sliced green onions, optional, for serving

## Instructions

### Step 1: Start the noodles

Cook the noodles according to the package directions. Drain and divide them among four
large bowls. If using cooked rice instead, divide it among the bowls.

### Step 2: Build the broth

While the noodles cook, bring the water to a gentle boil in a large pot. Add the ginger
and garlic, then simmer for 3-5 minutes to infuse the broth.

### Step 3: Cook the bok choy

Add the bok choy stems and simmer for 2-3 minutes. Add the leaves and cook for 1 minute
more. Stir in 1 tbsp soy sauce.

### Step 4: Cook the beef

Add the frozen beef strips in small batches, separating them with tongs. Swish the slices
through the simmering broth until fully cooked, about 1-2 minutes per batch. Do not leave
the beef frozen together in one thick clump.

### Step 5: Add the miso

Turn off the heat. Put the miso in a small bowl and whisk in 1-2 cups of hot broth until
smooth. Stir the miso mixture back into the pot. Taste and add the remaining soy sauce
only if needed.

### Step 6: Finish and serve

Stir in the sesame oil, then ladle the soup over the noodles or rice. Top with green
onions or chili crisp if desired.

## Athlete-Friendly Timing

- **2-3 hours before a hard workout:** Serve a full bowl with noodles or rice and the
  normal amount of beef and bok choy.
- **60-90 minutes before a workout:** Use a smaller portion of beef and bok choy, keep
  sesame oil and chili light, and emphasize noodles or rice.
- Have water with the meal. Miso and soy sauce provide a salty broth that works well for
  a sweaty training day.

## Variations & Substitutions

- **Use rice:** Replace the noodles with about 4 cups cooked rice.
- **No bok choy:** Substitute napa cabbage, spinach, or another quick-cooking green.
- **More heat:** Add chili crisp, chili oil, or a pinch of red pepper flakes at the table.
- **More aromatics:** Add sliced green onion, mushrooms, or a small piece of kombu while
  the broth simmers.

## Make-Ahead & Storage

Store the broth, beef, and vegetables separately from the noodles or rice when possible so
the starch does not absorb all the broth. Refrigerate promptly in shallow containers and
use within 3-4 days. Reheat the soup to a simmer and add fresh noodles or rice if available.

## Dietary Notes

Miso and soy sauce contain soy. Many Japanese noodles and standard soy sauces contain
wheat, so check labels or use verified gluten-free alternatives. Sesame oil contains sesame.
Check ingredient labels and cross-contact information for allergies.

## Safe Handling

Keep frozen beef cold until cooking and use clean utensils for raw meat. Cook the beef
until fully cooked throughout. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F)
and reheat to 165°F.

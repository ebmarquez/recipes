Creamy, hearty Tuscan soup with spicy Italian sausage, tender potatoes, and wilted kale in a
Parmesan cream broth. A home-kitchen sausage and potato soup with a creamy finish.

**Prep:** 10 minutes | **Cook:** About 33 minutes, plus bringing to a boil | **Total:** About 43
minutes or longer | **Yield:** 6 servings

## Ingredients

- 1 lb Italian sausage (hot or mild — casings removed)
- 4 slices bacon, chopped (or 4 oz pancetta)
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 medium yellow onion, diced
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- ½ tsp red pepper flakes (optional, for heat)
- 6 cups chicken broth
- 3 medium russet potatoes (~1.5 lb), sliced into ¼-inch half-moons
- 4 cups kale, stems removed, torn into bite-size pieces (about ½ bunch)
- 1 cup heavy cream
- Salt and black pepper to taste
- Grated Parmesan for serving
- Crusty bread for serving

**Substitutions:** Turkey sausage for a lighter version. Yukon Gold potatoes instead of russets
(hold their shape better). Half-and-half instead of heavy cream (slightly thinner). Spinach or
escarole for kale — add at the very end since they wilt faster. Skip bacon and use 2 tbsp olive oil
if you want.

## Instructions

1. **Crisp the bacon (5 min)** — In a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat, cook chopped bacon
   until crisp. Remove with a slotted spoon, leave the fat. Set bacon aside for topping.
2. **Brown the sausage (5 min)** — Add sausage to the bacon fat, break into crumbles. Cook until
   browned and at least 160°F internally (165°F for turkey sausage). Remove to a plate, leaving
   about 1 tbsp fat in the pot (drain excess if greasy).
3. **Build the base (3 min)** — Add olive oil and onion. Cook 2-3 min until softened. Add garlic and
   red pepper flakes — cook 30 seconds until fragrant.
4. **Simmer the potatoes (15 min)** — Pour in chicken broth and add potato slices. Bring to a boil,
   then reduce to medium and simmer 12-15 min until potatoes are fork-tender.
5. **Add greens and sausage (3 min)** — Return sausage to the pot, stir in kale. Simmer 3-4 min
   until kale is wilted and tender.
6. **Finish with cream (2 min)** — Reduce heat to low. Stir in heavy cream and warm through (don't
   boil hard or it can separate). Season with salt and pepper.
7. **Serve** — Ladle into bowls, top with crispy bacon and grated Parmesan. Crusty bread on the side
   for dunking.

## Chef's Tips

- **Don't boil after the cream** — keep it at a gentle simmer once the cream goes in to prevent
  curdling.
- **Slice potatoes thin and even** — ¼-inch half-moons cook fast and stay tender. Thicker slices
  need more time.
- **Kale > spinach here** — kale holds up to the broth and reheats without turning to mush. If using
  spinach, add it off the heat at the very end.
- **Render the fat for flavor** — building the soup in the bacon and sausage drippings is where the
  depth comes from. Don't drain it all.
- **Even better day two** — flavors deepen overnight. Reheat gently on low; add a splash of broth if
  it thickened up.
- **Freezing note** — the cream broth can separate slightly when frozen. If meal-prepping, freeze
  before adding cream and stir it in fresh when reheating.

## Dietary Notes

Cream and Parmesan contain milk; ordinary bread contains wheat. Check sausage and broth for gluten.
Check ingredient labels and cross-contact information for allergies.

## Cooking Safety

Pork/beef sausage needs 160°F and poultry sausage needs 165°F. Keep raw meat and its utensils
separate from ready-to-eat foods; wash hands and clean work surfaces after handling raw meat.

[Safe cooking temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).

## Safe Handling

Refrigerate promptly in shallow containers at 40°F or below, within 2 hours of preparation (1 hour
above 90°F). Use within 3-4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165°F; bring soups and sauces to a rolling
boil when reheating. Thaw frozen portions in the refrigerator, not on the counter.

[USDA leftover handling guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety).

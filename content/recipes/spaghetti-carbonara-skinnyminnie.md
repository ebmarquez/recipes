This cream-based pasta keeps the saved pancetta, yolk and Parmesan proportions, with careful heating
for a finished egg sauce.

## Ingredients

- 1 lb spaghetti
- Water for boiling
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 10 oz pancetta, cut into 1/2-inch pieces
- 1 cup heavy cream, plus additional cream if needed for thinning
- 4 large egg yolks
- 2 cups grated Parmesan (5 oz), plus additional cheese for serving
- 1 tsp salt
- 1/4 tsp ground black pepper

## Instructions

1. **Cook the pasta.** Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Add the spaghetti and cook to al dente,
   using the package timing; the saved estimate is about 8 minutes. Drain.
2. **Render the pancetta while the water heats.** Warm the olive oil in a heavy skillet over medium
   heat and add the diced pancetta. Start checking after 3-4 minutes but continue until fully cooked
   according to its package and browned. Keep both the meat and its rendered fat.
3. **Mix the sauce.** Whisk the measured cream, yolks, Parmesan, salt and pepper in a bowl.
4. **Combine away from direct heat.** Return the drained pasta to its pot off the burner. Add the
   egg-cream mixture in portions, tossing continuously, then add the warm pancetta and its fat.
5. **Finish with a thermometer.** Use very low heat as needed and keep stirring until the egg sauce
   reaches 160°F. Add a little of the extra cream if the sauce tightens, and recheck the temperature
   after additions. Serve immediately with extra Parmesan.

## Notes

- This is the cream-based version in the saved SkinnyMinnie record, which credits a Martha Stewart
  adaptation. No public original URL was supplied.
- Cream can soften the sauce's texture but does not prevent eggs from scrambling or prove they are
  safely cooked. Keep the heat low and the pasta moving.
- The ingredient record gives both 2 cups and 5 oz Parmesan. Use 5 oz as the repeatable amount
  because grating changes cup volume.
- Wheat, eggs and milk are present. Check the pancetta label for additional ingredients.
- See
  [FDA egg guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/what-you-need-know-about-egg-safety)
  for the 160°F egg-dish minimum.
- Keep raw or uncooked meat, its juices and utensils away from ready-to-eat ingredients. Wash hands
  and clean contact surfaces.
  [Safe cooking temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).

## Storage

Refrigerate leftovers in shallow covered containers at 40°F or below within 2 hours of cooking or
preparation, or within 1 hour when the surroundings exceed 90°F. Use within 3-4 days. Reheat to
165°F throughout, adding a little water or the recipe liquid if needed; use a thermometer rather
than time alone. Serve fresh when possible; the sauce can separate on reheating and is not a good
freezing candidate.

[USDA cooling, storage and reheating guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety).

Use a quart of pasteurized milk and plain live-culture yogurt for a small refrigerated batch;
results depend on the culture and temperature control.

## Ingredients

- 1 quart (4 cups) pasteurized milk, preferably whole
- 2 tbsp plain, unsweetened yogurt labeled with live active cultures
- Ice and water for an optional cooling bath; neither goes into the milk

## Instructions

1. **Check equipment before starting.** Use an Instant Pot with a working yogurt program and a food
   thermometer. Read the instructions for the exact model; this is not a pressure-cooking cycle.
   Wash the inner pot, lid, thermometer and utensils thoroughly, and use the manufacturer's cleaning
   or sanitizing directions rather than assuming a short improvised steam cycle sterilizes them.
2. **Heat the milk.** Put the quart of milk in the inner pot. Use the model's yogurt heating
   program, checking with the thermometer rather than relying on the completion beep. Reach
   180-185°F; if the model instructions allow additional gentle heating, stir and monitor closely to
   avoid scorching. Hold off the heat for 5 minutes.
3. **Cool before adding the culture.** Remove the inner pot and cool the milk to 105-115°F. An
   ice-water bath around the outside of the pot can shorten this to roughly 5-10 minutes; cooling
   without it can take about an hour. Keep bath water out and check the milk temperature directly.
   Dry the outside and base of the inner pot before returning it to the cooker. Lift off any surface
   skin if desired.
4. **Inoculate.** Blend the 2 tbsp yogurt with a little warm milk in a clean bowl, then stir it into
   the pot.
5. **Incubate under control.** Use the model's yogurt incubation setting for 8-10 hours, with the
   milk maintained around 110°F within the 105-115°F range. Do not substitute Keep Warm, pressure
   cooking, an unmeasured oven or an unregulated cooler. If your equipment cannot maintain the
   required range, do not improvise this process. Discard any batch after loss of temperature
   control or failure to set; do not taste it or try to rescue it.
6. **Chill promptly.** At the end of incubation, transfer to clean shallow food-safe containers and
   refrigerate at 40°F or below. Chill without stirring for at least 4 hours; a longer chill can
   improve texture. For thicker yogurt, strain for 1-2 hours through clean cheesecloth set over a
   bowl inside the refrigerator.

## Notes

- Whole milk generally gives more body; lower-fat pasteurized milk is an option. Do not use raw milk
  for this adaptation.
- A yogurt's brand name does not guarantee that a home batch will reproduce its texture or flavor.
  The culture, milk and heating history all matter.
- Use a fresh store-bought live-culture starter for each batch here. The saved claims about harmless
  low temperatures, indefinite timing extensions and a guaranteed number of reusable generations are
  not adopted.
- Read [the credited yogurt guide](https://amindfullmom.com/instant-pot-yogurt/) and locate the
  correct model in
  [Instant Pot's manual library](https://instantpot.com/pages/multi-cooker-product-manuals). The
  linked guide was consulted for temperature and timing facts; these directions are independently
  written.
- Milk is an allergen. Check the starter for additives, including gelatin if avoiding animal-derived
  ingredients, and for cross-contact information.

## Storage

Keep finished yogurt covered at 40°F or below and use within 3-4 days as a conservative household
window. Start refrigeration promptly after incubation; the controlled culturing period is not
permission to leave finished yogurt on the counter. Strain only under refrigeration. Do not judge a
questionable batch safe by tasting it.

[USDA refrigeration and cooling guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety).

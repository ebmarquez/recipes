An egg-and-cheese sauce coats the pasta without cream; a thermometer check replaces assumptions
about residual heat.

## Ingredients

- 12 oz spaghetti
- 6 quarts water
- 3 tbsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt for the pasta water
- 4 oz guanciale, pancetta or thick-cut bacon, cut into strips about 1 by 1/4 inch
- 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 2 large eggs
- 3 large egg yolks
- 2 oz finely grated Pecorino Romano or Parmesan; reserve one quarter for serving
- Freshly ground black pepper

## Instructions

1. **Start the water and eggs.** Bring the measured water and salt to a boil. In a bowl, whisk the
   eggs, yolks, three quarters of the cheese and plenty of pepper.
2. **Render the pork.** In a wide, heavy pot, cook the guanciale with the olive oil over medium heat
   for about 7-10 minutes, stirring as it browns. Follow the product's cooking requirements rather
   than assuming cured meat is ready to eat. Transfer the pieces to a dish. Pour the fat into a
   heatproof cup and return 3 tbsp to the pot.
3. **Partly cook the pasta.** Add spaghetti to the boiling water and cook until about 2 minutes
   short of the package's al dente timing. Save 1 3/4 cups cooking water before draining.
4. **Finish the pasta in the pan.** Bring 1 cup of the reserved water to a boil with the measured
   pork fat. Add the spaghetti and toss for about 2 minutes, until the pasta is tender and the
   liquid has reduced by roughly half.
5. **Temper and finish the sauce.** Remove the pot from the burner. Slowly whisk 1/4 cup of reserved
   water into the egg mixture, then pour it over the pasta while tossing continuously. Add the
   remaining pasta water in small amounts as needed. Return to very low heat only as necessary,
   continuing to toss, until the egg-containing sauce reaches 160°F. Do not assume that glossy sauce
   or hot noodles alone establish that temperature.
6. **Serve immediately.** Fold in the pork. Divide among bowls and add the reserved cheese and more
   pepper.

## Notes

- Move the pan off direct heat for the initial egg addition to reduce scrambling, then use measured,
  controlled heat to finish safely. If it thickens quickly, loosen with reserved water.
- The measured salt is Diamond Crystal kosher salt for 6 quarts of water, not a direction to add 3
  tbsp of table salt to the sauce.
- Guanciale, pancetta and bacon have different salt levels. Taste before adding anything beyond the
  listed salt.
- These directions include a conservative
  [FDA egg-dish temperature check](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/what-you-need-know-about-egg-safety);
  the source author's endorsement of this adaptation is not implied.
- Keep raw or uncooked meat, its juices and utensils away from ready-to-eat ingredients. Wash hands
  and clean contact surfaces.
  [Safe cooking temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).

## Storage

Refrigerate leftovers in shallow covered containers at 40°F or below within 2 hours of cooking or
preparation, or within 1 hour when the surroundings exceed 90°F. Use within 3-4 days. Reheat to
165°F throughout, adding a little water or the recipe liquid if needed; use a thermometer rather
than time alone. Best served fresh. Refrigeration and reheating can make the egg sauce grainy;
freezing is not recommended.

[USDA cooling, storage and reheating guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety).

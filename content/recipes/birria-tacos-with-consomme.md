A dried-chile beef braise supplies both the taco filling and a broth to serve alongside the crisped
tortillas.

## Ingredients

### Beef

- 4 lb chuck roast, cut into large chunks
- Salt and black pepper for the meat
- High-smoke-point cooking oil for optional searing

### Braising sauce

- 10 dried guajillo chiles, stems and seeds discarded
- 5 dried ancho chiles, stems and seeds discarded
- 4 dried arbol chiles, stems and seeds discarded
- 1 onion, quartered
- 3 Roma tomatoes
- 8 garlic cloves, peeled
- 3 bay leaves
- 1/2 Mexican cinnamon stick
- Water to cover the chiles and vegetables, plus extra as needed for the braise
- 1 cup reserved chile cooking water
- 2 cups beef broth
- 1/4 cup white vinegar
- 1 tbsp salt
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 1 tbsp garlic powder
- 2 tsp onion powder
- 2 tsp dried oregano
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- 1 tbsp ground cumin

### Taco assembly

- Corn tortillas, enough for 16-20 tacos
- Shredded Oaxaca cheese, or low-moisture mozzarella
- Finely chopped white onion
- Chopped cilantro
- Lime wedges

## Instructions

1. **Prepare the chile base.** In a saucepan, cover the dried chiles, quartered onion, tomatoes,
   garlic, bay leaves and cinnamon with water. Boil for about 10 minutes, until the chiles bend
   easily. Save 1 cup of the liquid and discard the bay leaves.
2. **Blend and strain.** Let the hot ingredients cool as needed for your blender's instructions.
   Blend the softened chiles, vegetables and cinnamon with the reserved water, broth and vinegar.
   Pass through a fine strainer, pressing with a spoon. Do not seal hot liquid inside a personal
   blender.
3. **Start the braise.** Season the beef with salt and pepper. If searing, heat a little oil in a
   Dutch oven and brown uncrowded batches for about 2-3 minutes per side. Put all beef in the pot
   with the strained sauce and the seven measured seasonings. Add water if needed to keep the meat
   nearly covered.
4. **Cook until it pulls apart.** Bring the sauce to a simmer, cover and lower the heat. Allow 3½-4
   hours, checking occasionally for drying or sticking and adding water as needed. The meat must
   reach at least 145°F with a 3-minute rest for safety, and this dish requires substantially more
   cooking until it shreds easily.
5. **Separate filling and broth.** Lift the beef out with a slotted spoon and shred it with forks.
   Moisten the filling with broth. Keep the remaining broth hot for dipping, and spoon some of its
   surface fat into a separate bowl.
6. **Crisp the tacos in batches.** Lightly coat a tortilla with the skimmed fat and lay it in a
   medium-hot skillet. Place cheese and beef on one half, add chopped onion and cilantro, then fold.
   Cook for about 2-3 minutes per side, adjusting the heat so the tortilla crisps without burning.
7. **Serve promptly.** Bring each batch to the table with lime and hot dipping broth rather than
   stacking all the tacos while they soften.

## Notes

- Use 2 arbol chiles for less heat, or more than 4 for a hotter version. Keep the guajillo and ancho
  amounts for the base flavor.
- The saved recipe estimates 16-20 tacos for 6-8 people. Cheese, tortilla size and filling portions
  affect the actual yield.
- Cool the braise overnight for easier fat separation, then reheat the meat and broth fully before
  assembling tacos.
- Pair with [Cabbage and Lime Slaw](../cabbage-lime-slaw/),
  [Vegetarian Mexican Rice](../vegetarian-mexican-rice/), or
  [Pickled Red Onions](../easy-pickled-red-onions/).
- Cheese contains milk; check corn tortillas, broth and seasonings for wheat or cross-contact.
- Keep raw or uncooked meat, its juices and utensils away from ready-to-eat ingredients. Wash hands
  and clean contact surfaces.
  [Safe cooking temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).

## Storage

Refrigerate leftovers in shallow covered containers at 40°F or below within 2 hours of cooking or
preparation, or within 1 hour when the surroundings exceed 90°F. Use within 4 days. Reheat to 165°F
throughout, adding a little water or the recipe liquid if needed; use a thermometer rather than time
alone. Freeze meat with broth for up to 3 months; thaw in the refrigerator. Fry the tortillas only
when serving.

[USDA cooling, storage and reheating guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety).
